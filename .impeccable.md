## Design Context

### Users
Austin locals looking for a wellness studio that feels like a neighborhood spot, not a chain gym. They're browsing on their phone between errands or on a laptop at home. Primary jobs: find a class that fits their schedule, understand what Prana offers, sign up for a membership, or book a drop-in. Secondary: check out the recovery amenities ("The Space"), learn who teaches, inquire about private events. The emotional context is aspirational-but-accessible: they want to feel better, not perform wellness.

### Brand Personality
**Intentional, grounded, warm.** Prana speaks like a friend who happens to run a really good studio. Not clinical, not hyper-motivational, not precious. The tagline — "Move with intention. Rest with purpose." — captures the cadence: calm confidence, no urgency. The brand is specific to this studio, this neighborhood, these people. Generic wellness language is the enemy.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Visual tone:** Warm editorial. Photography-forward, generous whitespace, earthy color blocking. Think a well-designed independent magazine about a place you'd actually go, not a fitness brand selling transformation.

**Palette:** Warm earth tones defined in tokens.css:
- Cream (`#EBE3D4`) and warm-white (`#F5EFE2`) as surfaces
- Espresso (`#574237`) and deep-brown (`#3A2A22`) for text
- Terracotta (`#B9755A`) as primary accent
- Sage (`#7C8C6E`) and golden (`#E8D5A8`) as secondary accents

**Typography:** Cormorant Garamond (display) + Inter (body). The pairing is already established and approved by the designer. Cormorant carries the warmth; Inter stays out of the way.

**Theme:** Light. The studio is full of natural light, warm wood, and plants. The site reflects that physical space. Dark mode is not planned.

**Motion:** Quiet and still. No parallax, no scroll-triggered reveals, no section entry animations. Only motion: hero video loop, hover transitions, nav overlay open/close, header mode color flip. All respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.

**References:**
- *Core Atelier Pilates* (coreatelierpilates.com) — warm editorial typography, earthy color blocking, photography as visual language, generous whitespace. Avoid its overuse of italic-word-in-every-headline and JS-rendered schedule.
- *CoreFit by Phenomenon Studio* (Dribbble concept) — functional UX patterns: class filter pills, schedule grid with color-coded time slots, tag-based metadata, pill buttons, stacked horizontal cards. Avoid its generic fitness copy, heavy glassmorphism, and cluttered hero.

**Anti-references:** Any generic AI-generated wellness site with a hero-stats-testimonials-CTA sandwich, stock gradients, or rounded-card grids. If the layout could belong to any wellness studio, it's wrong for Prana.

### Design Principles
1. **Prana-specific, not category-generic.** Every layout decision should feel like it was designed for this studio, not for "wellness studios" as a category. If a section could be swapped onto a competitor's site unchanged, it needs rethinking.
2. **Photography carries the design.** The studio's real photography (warm wood, natural light, real people) does the heavy visual lifting. The design supports and frames the images, not the other way around.
3. **Quiet confidence over loud persuasion.** No urgency tactics, no countdown timers, no "limited spots!" pressure. The site earns trust by being clear, warm, and easy to use. Let the studio sell itself.
4. **Every element earns its place.** No filler sections, no decorative-only components, no "nice to have" blocks. If it doesn't serve the visitor's job or reinforce the brand, it doesn't ship.
5. **Still, not animated.** Motion is reserved for functional state changes. The site's personality comes from typography, color, and composition, not from movement.
